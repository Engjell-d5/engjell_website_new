# Podcast pitch kit

The other half of the distribution fix. LinkedIn compounds slowly from a small
base. One good podcast placement puts you in front of thousands of the right
people in a single afternoon, and the episode keeps working for years.

You have the assets already: `/speaking`, a one-sheet, KosICT 2023, and a
podcast of your own, which matters more than it sounds. Hosts book people who
understand what a bad guest costs them.

---

## The angle to lead with

Not "founder of a tech company". Every host has forty of those in the inbox.

Lead with the thing only you can say:

> I built a company in a country where everyone with a track record had already
> emigrated, so I had to hire people with no experience and bring them up myself.
> That forced me to write down what most founders keep in their head, a decade
> before I would have chosen to. Now we run a marketplace placing vetted
> AI-native engineers from across the Balkans into companies in Europe, and the
> same lesson decides who succeeds with AI and who buys a tool that changes
> nothing.

Three things a booker hears in that: a place their audience has not heard from,
a contrarian mechanism, and a live commercial vantage point on AI hiring.

---

## Four pitchable topics

Give a host options. One topic reads as a one-trick guest, five reads as
unfocused.

**1. The founder is the bottleneck, and it is not about time management**
What stops when you stop. Why context, not skill, is usually the constraint, and
why it is the cheapest of the three to fix and the one nobody fixes.

**2. Why most AI projects change nothing**
The sequencing argument. Map, unify, build, then automate. Start at step four and
you get a pilot that demos beautifully and changes nothing. Concrete: five people
in recruitment became two.

**3. Hiring in a market everyone left**
Building an engineering org in the Balkans. What you learn when most of your team
is doing their first professional job, and why that turned out to be an advantage
rather than a handicap.

**4. What AI actually changed about hiring engineers**
The cost of a first draft went to zero, judgement did not. Why that means smaller
teams of better paid people, argued by someone whose business is placing them.

## The pitch email

Short, no attachments, one link. Bookers scan on a phone.

> **Subject:** Podcast guest, the founder bottleneck nobody names
>
> Hi {name},
>
> I listened to the episode with {specific guest} on {specific topic}. {One real
> sentence about what you actually thought, including where you disagreed.}
>
> I run division5, a marketplace placing vetted AI-native engineers from across
> the Balkans into companies in Europe. I started it in Albania in 2015, when
> essentially everyone with a track record had already emigrated, so I hired
> people with no experience and brought them up myself.
>
> That constraint taught me something I now see everywhere: the founder is
> almost always the bottleneck, and it is almost never about time management.
>
> Three angles I could bring your audience:
>
> - Why most AI projects change nothing, and the sequencing mistake behind it
> - What actually happens when you build an engineering team in a market
>   everyone left
> - What AI changed about hiring engineers, from someone who places them
>
> I host a podcast myself, so I know what a bad guest costs you. Happy to send
> anything that helps you decide: engjellrraklli.com/speaking
>
> Engjell

**Why it is built this way.** The first line proves you listened, which most
pitches fail. The credential is one sentence, not a CV. Three topics, not one.
The podcast-host line is the strongest trust signal you have and costs nine
words. One link.

**Do not** attach a PDF, write more than 200 words, or say "I would love to
share my journey".

## Where to aim

Ranked by fit rather than size. A 2,000-listener show whose audience is European
founders hiring engineers is worth more to you than a 200,000-listener general
business show.

**Tier 1, your buyers are listening**
- European startup and scale-up podcasts, especially German, Dutch and Nordic,
  where nearshore hiring is a live budget conversation
- Agency and consultancy owner shows. Their audience is service businesses with
  exactly the bottleneck you describe, and they buy engineers
- Engineering leadership shows, for the AI-and-hiring angle

**Tier 2, credibility that travels**
- Balkan and CEE tech ecosystem podcasts. Easiest yes, and the clip is
  reusable. KosICT 2023 is your proof you do this well
- Remote and distributed work shows

**Tier 3, only once you have three episodes out**
- Larger general business shows, which mostly book people who have already
  proven they interview well

**Practical rule.** Ten pitches a month, tracked. Expect one or two yeses at the
start. Follow up once after ten days and never again. Every episode makes the
next pitch easier, because you can open with a link instead of a claim.

## Before you send anything

Check `/speaking` answers the four questions a booker has, in this order: who is
this, what will they talk about, have they done this before, how do I book them.
If any of those takes more than one scroll, fix that before pitching.

The bios in `lib/speaking.ts` now carry the marketplace description, so anything
generated from them is current. If you pitch with an old bio, that is the version
that ends up in show notes for the next three years.

## What to do with an episode afterwards

This is where most people leave the value on the floor.

- Pull two or three 60 second clips and post them. That is a week of LinkedIn
  content you did not have to write
- Add the episode to `/media`, which is already your second best performing page
  at 59% engagement
- Send it to the newsletter. All 37 of them, which is small but they opted in
- Mention it in the next pitch. Momentum is the only thing that makes booking
  easier
